Insights / Three currencies

The exchange rate of a good life.

Money is the only currency with a printed number on it: which is why it gets all the attention. But every financial life actually runs on three, traded against each other daily.

The full story

Knowledge is the quiet compounder. A skill learned at 25 pays a dividend in every salary negotiation for four decades; financial literacy learned early prevents a lifetime of expensive mistakes. It costs mostly attention (the cheapest currency you own) and no market crash can redeem it away.

Time only runs one way. Every year that passes uninvested is a year of compounding permanently surrendered: the arithmetic behind why a five-year delay can cost more than a crore. Time cannot be earned back, only spent better.

Money is the convertible currency: invested well, it eventually buys back your time (the stage where work becomes a choice) and funds any knowledge you still want. Parked idle, it converts into nothing and quietly shrinks. The whole craft of wealth is running these exchanges deliberately: attention into knowledge, knowledge into income, income into invested money, and invested money (finally) into freedom.
